Attention is a moral choice

What you look at becomes who you are — and most of the internet is designed to make that choice for you.

21 August 2026 · attention · philosophy · focus

← All ideas

Most people talk about attention like a battery. Low charge, need a detox, back to normal.

That framing is too gentle. Attention is closer to a vote you cast every few seconds. You do not get those votes back. The tally is your life.

I am not arguing for monastic purity. I am arguing against the lie that “just scrolling” is empty. It is full. It fills you with other people’s urgency, other people’s envy, other people’s jokes that expire before dinner. Then you wonder why your own thoughts feel thin.

The cheap trick

Platforms do not steal attention the way a pickpocket steals a wallet. They rent it back to you as a feeling of being informed. You leave with the sensation of motion and the substance of fog.

Behavioral designers know this. A small reward at uneven intervals will keep a pigeon pecking longer than a steady reward. You are not a pigeon. You are also not immune to the same wiring.

So the ethical move is not shame. It is design — of your defaults. What opens when you unlock the phone. What sits on the home screen. What you refuse to negotiate with after 10pm.

A harder standard

Try this for a week: before you open anything that wants you, ask one question — Will this make my thinking sharper or softer for the next hour?

If you cannot answer, that is already an answer.

Philosophy that never touches your thumbs is decoration. The war room can dress an idea for the internet. Only you can decide whether the idea still owns you when the tab is closed.
