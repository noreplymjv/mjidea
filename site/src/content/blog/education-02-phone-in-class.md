The spreadsheet is not wrong. It is just not what moves people. With **phone in class**, the cost is rarely dramatic on day one. It is cumulative — like interest on a loan you did not mean to sign.

Younger people feel it as a low-grade panic that never quite becomes a sentence.

People reach for more motivation, more apps, more content. Rory-mode thinking says: change the choice architecture. Make the good option feel like taste, not virtue.

## What we keep getting wrong

If status, ease, and default settings run the show, then “try harder” is a weak product. **phone hotels + interesting problems** is a stronger one because it moves the easy path.

## What to do on Tuesday

Try it as a social object, not a private vow. Tell one person. Put it on a calendar. Remove one friction that makes the old habit win.

Younger people inherit systems older people built. Helping them means redesigning rooms, not blaming personalities.

If it only works when you feel inspired, it does not work.
