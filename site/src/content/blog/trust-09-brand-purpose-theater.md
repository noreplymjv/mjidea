We treat this like a knowledge gap. It is often a status gap. You can be informed about everything and intimate with almost nothing.

The problem shows up as **brand purpose theater** — ordinary, almost respectable, until you notice what it quietly spends.

The “quick check” lasts forty minutes and leaves a sticky aftertaste. We keep treating this like a personal failure of will. Willpower is a thin currency. Environments print their own money.

## Stop adding information

Here is the flip that matters: Solve for belonging first; the rest often follows. Information rarely loses to laziness. It loses to whatever feels easier, safer, or higher-status in the moment.

## A small redesign

A workable move is not a slogan. It is **do less claim more proof**. Make it visible. Make it slightly inconvenient to skip. Make it feel like taste when you keep it.

For younger generations especially, lectures about character bounce off interfaces built to harvest impulse. If you want different people, change what the room rewards — then practice the new ritual until it is boring.

None of this requires you to become a monk. It asks you to stop romanticizing heroics and start redesigning defaults. Make the good option the path of least resistance — then protect it.
