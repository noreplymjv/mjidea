Younger people did not invent this mess. They inherited the interface. Parents feel it when they realize the house is full of screens and empty of unhurried talk.

The problem shows up as **elite intern pipelines** — ordinary, almost respectable, until you notice what it quietly spends.

A calendar is packed, yet the week feels strangely uninhabited. We keep treating this like a personal failure of will. Willpower is a thin currency. Environments print their own money.

## The behavioral door

Here is the flip that matters: Reduce friction for the right act; add a little sand to the wrong one. Information rarely loses to laziness. It loses to whatever feels easier, safer, or higher-status in the moment.

## Make it easier to be decent

A workable move is not a slogan. It is **lottery + pay**. Make it visible. Make it slightly inconvenient to skip. Make it feel like taste when you keep it.

For younger generations especially, lectures about character bounce off interfaces built to harvest impulse. If you want different people, change what the room rewards — then practice the new ritual until it is boring.

None of this requires you to become a monk. It asks you to stop romanticizing heroics and start redesigning defaults. Put the better behavior where status already lives — or move status.
