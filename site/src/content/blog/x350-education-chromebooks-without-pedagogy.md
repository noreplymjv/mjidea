A good intention dies in the gap between the app icon and the door.

Most advice on this starts too late. With **chromebooks without pedagogy**, the cost is rarely dramatic on day one. It is cumulative — like interest on a loan you did not mean to sign.

The weird part is how normal it looks from the outside.

People reach for more motivation, more apps, more content. Rory-mode thinking says: change the choice architecture. Stop asking how to motivate people. Ask what the default makes easy.

## A different game board

If status, ease, and default settings run the show, then “try harder” is a weak product. **teacher-led tech** is a stronger one because it moves the easy path.

## Practice, not posture

Try it as a social object, not a private vow. Tell one person. Put it on a calendar. Remove one friction that makes the old habit win.

Younger people inherit systems older people built. Helping them means redesigning rooms, not blaming personalities.

Write the ritual down. Rituals beat moods.
